\begin{abstract}
Interpretable models are often assumed to be much less accurate than ensembles. On a standard
synthetic binary classification task with six informative features, a decision tree of depth 3,
readable at a glance, reaches a test accuracy of 0.900.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A depth-3 tree has at most eight leaves and can be audited by hand. We measure its accuracy on a
fixed synthetic task under a fully specified protocol.

\section{Method}
A CART decision tree with Gini impurity, maximum depth 3, no other stopping rule and no pruning,
random state equal to the seed of the split. Features are used as generated.

\section{Experimental setup}
\paragraph{Data.} One fixed sample of 2000 points from scikit-learn's
\texttt{make\_classification} with 20 features of which 6 are informative and 2 redundant,
2 classes, 5\,\% of the labels flipped at random (\texttt{flip\_y=0.05}), other parameters at
their defaults, generated with \texttt{random\_state=0}.
\paragraph{Splits.} For each seed $s \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, a stratified split with 30\,\% test
points (\texttt{train\_test\_split(test\_size=0.3, random\_state=s, stratify=y)}).
\paragraph{Metric.} Test accuracy, mean and sample standard deviation over the three seeds.
\paragraph{Software.} Python 3.12, scikit-learn 1.9.1, CPU only.
