\begin{abstract}
We train a multilayer perceptron with a single hidden layer of 64 units on the scikit-learn
digits dataset. It reaches a test accuracy of 0.972 and outperforms a Gaussian naive Bayes
classifier trained on the same splits by 0.126 accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Generative classifiers such as naive Bayes are fast and need no tuning, but their independence
assumption is a poor fit for pixel data. We measure how much a small discriminative network gains
over naive Bayes when both are trained on exactly the same data.

\section{Method}
\paragraph{MLP.} Features are standardized (zero mean, unit variance, training statistics only)
and fed to a multilayer perceptron with one hidden layer of 64 ReLU units, trained with Adam
(scikit-learn defaults: learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 200), L2 penalty $\alpha = 10^{-4}$,
for at most 300 epochs, with the network initialized from the same seed as the split.
\paragraph{Baseline.} Gaussian naive Bayes with default variance smoothing, on raw pixels.

\section{Experimental setup}
\paragraph{Data.} The digits dataset shipped with scikit-learn (\texttt{load\_digits}): 1797
images of $8 \times 8$ pixels, 10 classes.
\paragraph{Splits.} For each seed $s \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, a stratified split with 30\,\% test
samples (\texttt{train\_test\_split(test\_size=0.3, random\_state=s, stratify=y)}). Both models
are trained and evaluated on the same split for a given seed.
\paragraph{Metric.} Test accuracy, mean and sample standard deviation over the three seeds. The
gain is the per-seed difference between the two models, averaged over seeds.
\paragraph{Software.} Python 3.12, scikit-learn 1.9.1, CPU only.

\section{Results}
Table~\ref{tab:digits-mlp} lists both models. The MLP reaches a test accuracy of
0.972 $\pm$ 0.006, and it outperforms Gaussian naive Bayes by 0.126 accuracy on average over the
three seeds.

\section{Conclusion}
A single hidden layer of 64 units is enough to beat naive Bayes by a wide margin on digits.
