\begin{abstract}
We argue that depth-limited decision trees offer both interpretability and accuracy on small
image tasks. On the scikit-learn digits dataset a decision tree of depth 8 reaches a test
accuracy of 0.910 and beats standardized logistic regression by 0.040 accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Linear models are the usual interpretable baseline, but their weights are hard to read for pixel
inputs. We compare a depth-limited tree with logistic regression on handwritten digits.

\section{Method}
\paragraph{Decision tree.} A CART decision tree with Gini impurity and maximum depth 8, no other
stopping rule, random state equal to the seed, on raw pixels.
\paragraph{Baseline.} Multinomial logistic regression with an L2 penalty ($C = 1.0$), L-BFGS for
at most 2000 iterations, on features standardized with training statistics.

\section{Experimental setup}
\paragraph{Data.} The digits dataset shipped with scikit-learn (\texttt{load\_digits}): 1797
images of $8 \times 8$ pixels, 10 classes.
\paragraph{Splits.} For each seed $s \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, a stratified split with 30\,\% test
samples (\texttt{train\_test\_split(test\_size=0.3, random\_state=s, stratify=y)}); both models
use the same split.
\paragraph{Metric.} Test accuracy, mean and sample standard deviation over the three seeds; the
gain is the per-seed difference averaged over seeds.
\paragraph{Software.} Python 3.12, scikit-learn 1.9.1, CPU only.

\section{Results}
Table~\ref{tab:digits-tree} reports the comparison. The depth-8 decision tree beats
standardized logistic regression by 0.040 accuracy, and it reaches a test accuracy of
0.910 $\pm$ 0.008.

\section{Conclusion}
Depth-limited trees need not trade accuracy for interpretability on digits.
